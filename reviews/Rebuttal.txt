We received three reviews, two of which were highly supportive and
awarded the proposal the highest possible score, while Reviewer 3
provided a substantially more critical assessment.

While Reviewer 3 raises a number of concerns regarding the motivation,
feasibility, and resourcing of the project, these concerns stand in
marked contrast to the assessments of Reviewers 1 and 2, who
independently describe the proposal as “excellent” and “top-notch”,
with “clear and coherent” methodology and deliverables that “would be
very valuable if achieved".

Moreover, several criticisms in Review 3 appear to stem from
misunderstandings of the proposal or contain factual inaccuracies,
which we would like to clarify below.

The most concrete concerns are factual inaccuracies in Review 3. The
review states that in WP7 “no specific collaborators are identified”
and that the proposal mentions “non-invertible symmetries” but “does
not provide citations.” Both statements are incorrect: WP7 explicitly
names Schreiber and Schenkel as collaborators, and the relevant
discussion is supported by citations in the proposal. Review 3 also
states that the investigators supervise “7 PDRAs and 13 PhD students
collectively.” This is factually incorrect: Kraus currently has 2
PDRAs and one long-term visitor (Ulrik Buchholtz), and the
investigators supervise 5 PhD students. In any case, existing staff
and PhD students are committed to other projects and are not a
substitute for dedicated research capacity on this proposal.

More broadly, several of the conceptual criticisms in Review 3 are
difficult to reconcile both with other parts of the same review and
with the assessments of Reviewers 1 and 2. Review 3 characterises the
proposal as “closer to an incremental extension (HoTT+) rather than a
fundamentally new direction”, while at the same time repeatedly
emphasising the technical difficulty and complexity of the proposed
programme. By contrast, Reviewer 1 explicitly states that “the
difference between (∞,1)-categories and (∞,n)-categories is closely
related to the difference between undirected and directed type
theories which forms the core of this proposal,” while Reviewer 2
emphasises that “the importance of direction in homotopy theory and
type theory is not well understood, but has consequences for pretty
much every topic in mathematics and computer science.”

Review 3 also repeatedly criticises the proposal for being
foundational and theoretical, and for lacking immediate industrial
applications. However, the proposal is explicitly a foundational
theoretical computer science project, whose primary aim is to develop
new mathematical and typetheoretic foundations. In this context, the
absence of short-term industrial deliverables should not be viewed as
a weakness. Indeed, many advances in formal verification and proof
assistants originated in similarly foundational work, including
Homotopy Type Theory itself. Reviewer 1 explicitly highlights the
growing importance of foundational type theory in formalised
mathematics and verification, while Reviewer 2 notes that the project
could become “hugely impactful in many areas.”

Review 3 further states that “no convincing case studies or motivating
examples are provided.” We do not believe this accurately reflects the
contents of the proposal. The proposal explicitly discusses several
motivating directions and use cases, including parametricity and
coherence, generalised inductive types, synthetic higher category
theory, and applications to mathematics and theoretical physics, in
particular higher quantum computation and non invertible
symmetries. Naturally, as a foundational theoretical project, these
applications are intended as mathematically substantial case studies
rather than industrial applications. In fact, Reviewer 2 explicitly
notes that “the importance of direction in homotopy theory and type
theory is not well understood, but has consequences for pretty much
every topic in mathematics and computer science,” and further comments
that “If successful, the outcome will be hugely impactful in many
areas.”

Review 3 also questions the need for dedicated research staffing,
suggesting that “the investigators, together with existing
collaborators and PhD students, could achieve most of project goals
without additional staffing.” We do not believe this reflects either
the scope of the proposed programme or the realities of research
funding and supervision. The project consists of seven interconnected
work packages spanning semantics, metatheory, implementation,
parametricity, higher inductive types, and applications to mathematics
and physics over a four year period. Existing staff, collaborators,
visitors, and PhD students are funded for distinct projects and cannot
simply be reassigned to deliver the objectives of this
proposal. Moreover, this assessment contrasts sharply with Reviewer 2,
who considered the requested staffing comparatively modest, commenting
that “One postdoc between the three applicants … could have been two.”

In summary, while we appreciate Reviewer 3’s engagement with the
proposal, we believe that a substantial number of the concerns raised
either stem from factual inaccuracies or do not accurately reflect the
aims and scope of the proposed research. In several key areas,
including the significance of Directed Type Theory, the clarity of the
workplan, the appropriateness of the requested resources, and the
existence of motivating applications - the assessment in Review 3
stands in direct contrast to the independently written evaluations of
Reviewers 1 and 2, both of whom strongly endorse the proposal as
ambitious, timely, feasible, and potentially highly impactful. We
therefore hope that the panel will place particular weight on the
consistency of the positive expert assessments together with the
clarifications provided above when evaluating the proposal as a whole.
